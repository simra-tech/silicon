# joint_r4_mc_20260928: joint calibrated mismatch screen of the TRIP chain with the r4 comparator clock (24 seeds, paired with r3)

This is simulated Monte Carlo mismatch, not hardware measurement. 24 seeds are not a yield qualification.

This is the r3 screen `../joint_r3_mc_20260926/` rerun with the r4 TRIP clock (candidate `nf4_novclk`: `XCLKI`
replaced by the non-overlap generator `g1_novclk`). Everything else is identical: the same 24 seeds, the same
calibration procedure, the same guard and residual probes (including 125 C), the same watchdog and the same pass
criteria. For every device that r3 also has, each seed draws exactly the same mismatch values as in r3 (checked
for 24 of 24 seeds, see "Pairing").

- **The 125 C hard drift of r3 is gone.** None of the 23 calibrated seeds trips at 24.5 mV or misses 25.5 mV at
  125 C, on either comparator. The four r3 failures (79003, 79009, 79013, 79018) pass, with the same draws.
- **What "residual" bounds.** The residual code sits half an LSB (+0.098 mV shunt) above the room crossing, as in r3,
  and the probes sit at 25.0 +/- 0.5 mV. A correct pair therefore bounds the calibrated threshold's shift from 25 C
  to within about -0.6 / +0.4 mV of shunt. The screen does not measure the shift more finely. In this sense the
  worst residual is < 0.5 mV in every calibrated seed and at every temperature (r3: > 0.5 mV in 4 seeds at 125 C).
- **Where the hard offset went.** Per seed, the hard room crossing moved by -39 to -42 LSB (median -41) from r3

  now centred like the soft one: mean -0.07 mV, spread 0.92 mV (its own mismatch). The soft crossing is unchanged
  in 16 of 21 seeds calibrated in both runs, and 1 LSB (0.196 mV) higher in 5.
- **Calibration** reached a bracket in 23 of 24 seeds. None was clipped. 79007 and 79020 calibrated here; in r3
  they hit the watchdog.
- **Numerical failure, 79021.** Probe p00 (codes [0,0], 25 C, 25 mV) hit the 1200 s watchdog at t = 557.8 ns of

  ran p01 in 820 s. So this is a slow solve for this seed and deck, not CPU starvation. As in r3, there is no
  automatic retry, and the seed counts as a numerical failure.
  - An informational rerun of that deck, without the watchdog and on the same CPU, completed in about 1940 s. It
    passed the sampling rule and gave the expected decisions (soft trip, hard trip). This rerun is not counted.
  - In r3, 79021 passed (p00 took 535 s).

## What was substituted, and why not the extraction

The r3 screen does **not** use a TRIP extraction. Its TRIP is the schematic netlist `sources/trip.spice`
(`f5f0a90a…`), with `mm_ok=1` on every device. The r3 README lists "mismatch of the parasitic (PEX) netlists" as
not run. Substituting the r4 extraction (`sim/postlayout/g1_trip_nf4_novclk_pex.spice`, `6f518e07…`) would
therefore not be one-to-one:
- It is flat, so the 8 141 hierarchical TRIP parameter queries, the NF4 geometry check and the saved internal nodes
  (`xt.xch.*`) of the deck would not resolve.
- It has no `mm_ok`.
- It would add parasitics that r3 does not have.

So the r4 **schematic** TRIP was substituted at the same abstraction level. It is the r4 LVS reference
`layout/candidates/nf4_novclk/g1_trip_nf4_novclk_lvs.cdl` (`8943a5a8…`), which is "r3 NF4 TRIP CDL with XCLKI
replaced by g1_novclk", in the r3 source format. Mismatch of the r4 extraction is **not run**.

Changes to the r3 screen, all of them (`runner_r4mc.py --selftest` asserts the deck part):

| Item | r3 | r4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sources/trip.spice` | `f5f0a90a…` | `26fca47e…` = r3 file plus a 4-line header; `.subckt g1_trip_r4mc` with ports `cmp_clk_s cmp_clk_n` appended; `XCS … cmp_clk_s …`; `XCLKI vss clki_placeholder vdd vss g1_inv`; `g1_novclk` appended = `layout/candidates/nf4_novclk/g1_novclk.spice` (`1750146f…`) with `mm_ok=1` on its 20 devices |
| Deck, `XT` line | `… vdd vdda 0 g1_trip` | `… vdd vdda 0 xt.cmp_clk_s xt.cmp_clk_n g1_trip_r4mc` |
| Deck, after `Xbgr` | — | `Xnov clk xt.cmp_clk_s xt.cmp_clk_n vdd 0 g1_novclk` |
| Deck, NON_BGR parameter prints (BEFORE and AFTER) | 8 141 TRIP + SENSE entries | the same, plus 80 (`@n.xnov.<dev>.nsg13_lv_[np]mos[w,l,delvto,factuo]`, 20 devices) appended |
| Parameter vector | 11 512 | 11 592 (`phase_parameters` copied into the runner with the count changed) |
| `sources/bgr.spice`, `sources/sense.spice`, deck template, contract, sampling rule, watchdog, calibration rule, guards, residuals, temperatures | — | identical (same sha256) |

Connectivity is that of the r4 CDL:
- the generator input is the macro clock;
- `cmp_clk_s` clocks the soft comparator;
- `cmp_clk_n` clocks the hard comparator;
- the generator supply is VDD.

Two netlist choices exist only to keep the per-seed draw identical to r3. ngspice draws the `agauss` values in
instance order, and every MOS draws whether `mm_ok` is 1 or 0:
- The r3 inverter `XCLKI` stays in place with its input tied to vss and its output on an otherwise unused net. It
  consumes the same 8 draws. Electrically it is an idle inverter on the ideal 1.2 V supply, with no load on any
  clock net.
- The generator is instantiated at the top level after the BGR, so its 20 devices draw after every r3 device. The
  top-level nets are named `xt.cmp_clk_s` / `xt.cmp_clk_n`, so the saved vector `v(xt.cmp_clk_n)` (the hard
  clock used by the sampling rule) and the 18 wave columns are unchanged.

**Pairing.** `pairing_all.py` compares each seed's `parameters_before.json` (bulk) with r3's after dropping the 80
generator entries. Result: all 11 512 r3 entries are identical, value for value, for **24 of 24 seeds**.
`check_draw_pairing.py` showed the same for 79001 on an op-only deck before launch.

## Runs, CPUs and incidents

- All runs: `flow/launch_pinned.sh`, one seed per CPU on CPUs 96-119. The seed on CPU 96+i is 79001+i. The
  results root is `${BULK}/r4-20260927/jointmc`.
- **Launch** (`launch.sh`) 2026-09-27 22:18:47 CEST at nice 0. All 24 seeds ran concurrently.
- **Last seed finished** 2026-09-28 04:51 CEST, a wall time of 6 h 32 min.
  - Probe wall time: median 738 s, range 372-1200 s. The per-seed core time is in the table (4.8-6.4 h); 126.9
    core-hours in total, counting finished probes only.
  - Probes ran about 1.3-1.5 times slower than in r3 (about 600 s). The SMT siblings of CPUs 96-119 (32-55) carried
    other ngspice runs of this account, and a foreign multi-threaded job whose affinity includes 96-127 occupied
    single CPUs at times. At launch, CPUs 112 and 114-118 were busy.
  - No watchdog timeout other than 79021 p00 occurred.
- **Order of the probes.** At 23:25 the projected finish was close to the 06:00 deadline, and the 125 C residual
  probes run last. `switch_residuals_first.sh` therefore restarted every seed that was still calibrating with
  `runner_r4mc.py --resume --residuals-first`:
  - it waited for a new probe to start (< 45 s old), stopped that runner and its ngspice, and relaunched;
  - residuals now run before guards;
  - the probes, decks and pass criteria are unchanged.
- **Switch incident.** Two things went wrong:
  - The switch script was started as a background job of the interactive zsh, so its relaunches ran at **nice 5**
    for 1-10 minutes.
  - `--resume` treats a leaf whose ngspice was killed as terminal, because it has a `run.json` with returncode -15.
    Every switched seed therefore ended at once with a numerical calibration failure on the leaf it had just
    killed.

  The repair (`relaunch.sh`, 23:36:28, nice 0, same CPUs, `--resume --residuals-first`):
  - The 23 killed leaves (wall < 60 s, rc -15) were renamed `pNN.killed` in bulk.
  - The resumed seeds re-analysed their finished leaves p00-p04 or p00-p05, which have identical decks
    (`reused_leaves` in each summary), and re-simulated the rest.
  - No result comes from a niced or killed process: every niced probe was killed and re-simulated at nice 0.
  - The as-launched runner (without `--residuals-first`, `dddd19ae…`) is kept in bulk. The runner here adds only
    that option.
- 79021 stayed on the original launch. It ended after p01 with the numerical calibration failure described above.
